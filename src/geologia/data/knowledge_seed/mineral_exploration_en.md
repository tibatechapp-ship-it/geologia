# Mineral exploration — evidence and QA/QC

Original GEOLOGIA preparation checklist. A mineral occurrence, exploration target, and mineral resource/reserve are not interchangeable categories.

## Evidence to collect
- Geological mapping, lithology, alteration, structure, and regional context.
- Geochemical sample ID, coordinates/CRS, sample medium, collection method, field duplicate, preparation, analytical laboratory, method, detection limits, units, and assay certificate.
- QA/QC insertion and review: blanks, certified reference materials, duplicates, contamination checks, batch acceptance criteria, and chain of custody.
- Geophysical survey type, instrument, line/grid spacing, orientation, elevation, processing, and inversion assumptions.
- Drilling collars, downhole surveys, core recovery, intervals, logging standards, and database validation if drilling exists.

## Preflight
1. Check sample identity and coordinate integrity; do not join tables on ambiguous names.
2. Harmonize units and detection-limit notation; retain below-detection values with their qualifiers.
3. Review QA/QC failures before mapping or ranking anomalies.
4. Separate observed assays from interpolated surfaces and exploration hypotheses.
5. Record the vintage and completeness of historical occurrence databases.

## Safety boundary
An anomaly is a follow-up hypothesis, not proof of an economic deposit. Mineral resource/reserve reporting requires appropriate professional standards, drilling, sampling, estimation, and independent qualified-person review.
